Professional Profile

I am Aqab Zahoor, a PhD researcher in Aerospace and Ocean Engineering at Virginia Tech specializing in computational materials engineering, finite element analysis, and materials degradation modeling.

My research develops MOOSE-based models of stress corrosion cracking and corrosion fatigue, combining crystal plasticity, phase-field mechanics, and mechanochemical damage with LPBF processing and microstructural characterization. I also build multiscale workflows that transfer macroscopic loading histories into EBSD-informed microscale models.

01

Crystal Plasticity Finite Element Modeling (CPFE)

Crystal-scale constitutive modeling is used to resolve orientation-dependent slip, stress concentrations, and heterogeneous deformation. EBSD-informed microstructures connect crystallographic texture to local strain and damage evolution.

  • Single-crystal responses for [100], [110], and [111] orientations
  • Microstructure-informed stress and strain localization
  • Coupling with macroscale anisotropic plasticity
SCC Phase FieldUpload at assets/research/phase-field.png

02

Phase-Field Modeling of Stress Corrosion Cracking

A coupled electro-chemo-mechanical phase-field framework represents crack evolution in corrosion-sensitive alloys. The formulation links damage to stress-dependent passive-film rupture and repassivation kinetics.

  • Environmentally assisted degradation
  • Passive-film damage and repassivation
  • Crack evolution in LPBF and reinforced microstructures
Fatigue & Crack PropagationUpload at assets/research/corrosion-fatigue.png

03

Corrosion Fatigue and Crack Propagation

Mechanochemical modeling examines how cyclic loading, local chemistry, and reinforcing particles influence crack initiation and propagation. Critical stress and strain histories are used to identify degradation hotspots and fatigue-sensitive regions.

  • Cyclic damage and crack-driving mechanisms
  • Coupled corrosion–mechanical degradation
  • Microstructure-sensitive propagation paths
LPBF SS316L–Al₂O₃Upload at assets/research/lpbf-composite.png

04

Laser Powder Bed Fusion of SS316L–Al₂O₃ Composites

Computational and experimental work investigates how reinforcement content, particle size, and agglomeration affect strength and stress corrosion cracking in laser powder bed fused 316L composites.

  • 0.5–4 wt% Al₂O₃ reinforcement studies
  • Powder feedstock preparation and characterization
  • Processing–microstructure–property relationships
Macro–Micro MOOSEUpload at assets/research/multiscale-moose.png

05

Multiscale Finite Element Modeling using MOOSE

A macro-to-micro finite element workflow calibrates Hill anisotropic plasticity, identifies critical loading regions, and transfers local loading histories to EBSD-informed crystal plasticity and damage models.

  • Reusable MOOSE-based modeling workflows
  • Hill anisotropic plasticity calibration
  • Macro-to-micro load transfer and hotspot analysis
